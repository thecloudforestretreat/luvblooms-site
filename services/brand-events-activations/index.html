Designed with purpose

Built to support the brand, the room, and the camera.

Brand florals should do more than decorate a venue. They should reinforce the visual language of the campaign, create recognizable moments for guests, and photograph naturally without competing with the product or message.

We interpret the brief, palette, venue, audience, and desired level of impact as one system. The result may be restrained and architectural or abundant and sculptural, but each decision is tied to the purpose of the experience.

A useful brief

What helps us understand a brand project.

Begin with the event date, Miami-Dade location, guest profile, venue access, working budget, and the moments where flowers need to contribute. Brand guidelines, campaign artwork, product packaging, floor plans, and reference imagery help us understand the visual system.

If some details are still developing, send what is confirmed. Early context allows us to identify sourcing, production, and installation considerations before the design becomes too fixed.

FAQ

Questions about brand event florals.

What types of brand events are a fit?

Product launches, press previews, client dinners, retail pop-ups, branded hospitality, content productions, and other intentional guest experiences may be a fit. Scope is reviewed individually.

Can the florals follow brand colors?

Yes. We can interpret an established palette while keeping the flowers dimensional and natural. Exact flower varieties depend on seasonality, availability, and the approved direction.

Can you design around products or campaign materials?

Yes. Product packaging, campaign artwork, signage, furniture, lighting, and venue finishes can inform color, scale, material, and placement so the florals feel integrated.

Do you provide on-site styling?

Delivery, placement, and on-site styling can be included when the venue, timing, and scope require it. Access windows and production responsibilities should be shared early.

How far ahead should we inquire?

Earlier is better for installations, multiple floral moments, venue coordination, or specific sourcing. Send the confirmed date and known scope even when parts of the brief are still developing.

What should be included in the inquiry?

Include the date, venue, guest count, event purpose, working budget, desired floral moments, brand references, and the main contact responsible for approvals.
